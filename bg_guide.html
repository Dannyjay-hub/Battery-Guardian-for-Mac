Battery Guardian Guide

Understanding Forensic Diagnostics & Health Grading (v{{VERSION}})

Battery Guardian was built to uncover the hidden truth about your MacBook's battery. While macOS provides a simple "Normal" or "Service Recommended" label, Battery Guardian goes deeper by analyzing the raw hardware data from the TI Smart Battery System gas gauge chip using physics-based forensic checks.

Why is this necessary? Reprogrammed battery controllers can report plausible health and cycle data. Battery Guardian looks for supported contradictions and known reset patterns in the wider telemetry, while clearly reporting when the evidence is incomplete.

1. Health Score and Forensic Verdict

The health score summarises physical condition and forensic penalties. It must be read separately from the evidence verdict:

  • NO ANOMALIES: Supported checks found no contradiction; this is not proof of Apple provenance.
  • SUSPICIOUS: The active policy found a lower-confidence contradiction that needs review.
  • SPOOFED: Strong profile-supported evidence crossed the policy threshold.
  • INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE: The gauge is unsupported or required telemetry is missing.
  • ERROR: The scan did not complete, so no authenticity conclusion was made.

2. Core Hardware Metrics

The six metric cards show raw values pulled directly from the TI chip via ioreg. These are unsmoothed, unaveraged hardware readings.

Cycles

The cycle counter reported by the gauge. It becomes meaningful evidence only when compared with compatible records.

Health

The precise ratio of current maximum capacity to design capacity, parsed directly from AppleRawMaxCapacity, not the software-smoothed value shown in System Settings.

Entropy

The capacity gap between reported per-cell Qmax values. It is useful context but is not a universal pass/fail rule.

Operating Time

TotalOperatingTime is powered-on time. It is not the SBS manufacture date and is never presented as one.

Write Ratio

DataFlashWriteCount ÷ CycleCount. Shows how intensively the chip's memory has been written relative to cycle history. A useful secondary indicator for chip activity.

Temp

Live thermistor reading converted from deciKelvin. One reading alone is not an authenticity test.

3. The 9 Forensic Signals

The project models nine possible signals: reset signature, Qmax consistency, capacity relationship, DOD0 records, clock integrity, calibration timeline, pack voltage, historical continuity, and DataFlash writes. Only the subset active for the detected gauge profile can affect a verdict.

Observation only

Qmax Consistency and Capacity Relationship

The gauge may learn per-cell Qmax values and report usable capacity separately. These records are useful context, but fixed five-cycle, 1%, and 3% authenticity thresholds are not universally validated across TI models and learning states.

Active for bq20z451

Combined Reset Signature

A cycle count above the policy minimum, all DOD0 cells at the model-specific reset sentinel, and a zero DataFlash write count form the strongest labelled signature in the current Intel evidence. No component is treated as proof by itself.

Active when mapped

Calibration Timeline

Where the profile maps compatible fields, CycleCountLastQmax greater than CycleCount is a contradictory history and contributes to the forensic score.

Active when mapped

Clock Integrity

Where both counters are available, the policy compares TemperatureSamples with TotalOperatingTime. Thresholds are versioned empirical policy, not claims made by TI.

Disabled or observation-only

Pack Voltage and Historical Continuity

Pack voltage needs Mac model/topology profiles. Historical clock continuity needs proof that the gauge was powered between scans. Neither currently classifies a battery on its own.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Does NO ANOMALIES mean my battery is Apple-original?
No. It means the supported checks found no contradiction in the available telemetry.

Q: How often should I scan?
Once is enough to get a verdict. The Automation tab lets you schedule daily silent scans, useful for tracking degradation over time or building a history log before selling a laptop.

Q: Will Battery Guardian drain my battery?
No. Reading the IORegistry via ioreg is a zero-impact system call, equivalent to reading a text file. The entire scan completes in under a second and uses no measurable battery power.

Q: Why is there no Battery Made date?
TotalOperatingTime measures powered-on hours, not calendar age. Battery Guardian will show a manufacture date only after it directly reads and decodes the SBS manufacture-date field.

Q: Can a third-party or reprogrammed battery pass?
Yes. This is a forensic classifier built from available telemetry, documentation, and a still-small labelled dataset. It should be used as one source of evidence, not as a cryptographic certificate of origin.
